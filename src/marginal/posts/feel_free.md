---
title: "Feel Free"
date: "2005-01-22"
---

<p>"I'm not afraid of dying. I just don't want to be there when it happens."</p><div align="right">Woody Allen</div><hr>I'm surprised how often I see people cringe when the subject of dying comes up in conversation. As if somehow the discussion will hurry the day of our demise.<p></p>

<p>Of course, there are a lot of things I'd rather not talk about. Right at the top of that list would be "feelings". It's not that I am afraid of my feelings. I just don't want to be there when they happen. </p>

<p>To be clear, I'm not talking about the emotional moments when a performance by one of the kids takes my breath away, or when I'm stunned all over again by how much Katy loves me, or even when I can't finish the hymn we are singing on Sunday morning because I'm overcome by God's kindness.</p>

<p>I'm talking about the soul searching and self-assessment required when some well-meaning person asks, "So, how do you <i>feel</i> about this?" Like I have a clue. And do I have to feel something about everything? Somehow that idea steals something away from the importance of feelings in the first place. If feelings were that easy to sum up in words, would they be as meaningful as we know they are?</p>

<p>It's like printing and framing every snapshot you click off with your high-res digital camera. Admit it. Some are destined for the delete button. Others might represent a nice enough subject, but not the moment that matters or the candid that captures the essence. Even the best are only a dim reflection of reality.</p>

<p>So you see, I don't mind being there when death happens. But when it does, I sure don't want to feel it.<br></p><p></p>
