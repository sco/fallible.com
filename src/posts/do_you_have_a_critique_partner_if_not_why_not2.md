      alisa---oh, my goodness, do I ever love your coffee-themed blog! I had my hubby take a yummy swallow of its java goodness, too...
      
      I do know quite a number of published authors, but I wouldn't ask any of them to be my critique partner. They're too far ahead of me, I think, and it's unlikely that I could benefit them as much as they could me. (Although my author friend Lisa Samson does offer paid critique services, which I have taken advantage of...)
      
      I think it's probably most productive if you find another writer who's at approximately the same level you are, published or unpublished. Please understand I'm trying this arrangement for the first time myself! Kath and I have agreed to try, and if it just isn't working for one or both of us, to move on. It's a little scary, and so far one of us might be short $20, but no real harm done...  :)  Do you know if there's a writers group in your area that you could join, to meet other writers and possible critique partners?? I hope so!
      
      eija--Who is the intended audience for your &quot;religious&quot; writings? If they are for a &quot;general&quot; audience, having them read by non-Christians would be quite helpful, I'd think. Have you asked your non-Christian friends to give it a try for you? I edited a novel a couple of years ago for a male author. I've gotta think this was the most guy novel in the universe! Lots of lingo about construction sites, and war zones in Vietnam, and chemical warfare, etc. Even though I didn't exactly understand the content, I was able to edit the work so that it WAS understandable to his intended audience.
      Sometimes, ignorance of the subject is quite useful!
      
      Ame--I wish I'd latched on to the whole critique partner thing YEARS ago. But, alas, I am a slow learner, and have (in the past) been much more sensitive to criticism than I am now. I had a paid critique this weekend with an agent who prefaced his comments by saying he's known in the industry for being &quot;brutal.&quot; Sure, my hands started sweating, but I can take it now! The thing is, if we writers don't put ourselves in the position of receiving (good, strong, helpful) critique, we won't grow as writers nearly as fast as we might. So, submission it is!! I am glad critique is helping you along your path, also....
      
      Jody--Welcome! How do you know our dear Kath? Yes, timing (God's, in particular) is everything. I think I'm getting old enough that even taking God's timing into consideration, I have to come to the conclusion that it's pretty much now or never! (Yeah. I'm that old....)  :)  Best to you as you obey his calling on your life.

I sat in on a helpful session today with chick-lit author <a href="http://www.kristinbillerbeck.com/">Kristin Billerbeck</a> and romantic suspense author <a href="http://www.colleencoble.com/">Colleen Coble</a>. These two have been critique partners for the past seven years, trading chapters back and forth on an almost daily basis.

During that length of time, their careers have really taken off, and they take delight in having helped each other find such wonderful success.

My travel buddy Kath Atwell and I have decided to give this arrangement a try. We've agreed to be honest and hold each other accountable, unless or until it stops working for one or both of us.

I'm excited. I've never tried having a critique partner, and I think it might provide the boost both Kath and I have needed.

We haven't signed in blood or exchanged rings or anything, but she did offer me a bite of her Krispy Kreme. Sounds like a commitment to me!
